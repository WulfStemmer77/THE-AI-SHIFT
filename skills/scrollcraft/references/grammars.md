# Page grammars

A grammar is the organising logic of the entire page. Pick one primary grammar. The bans are part of the definition.

## Filmic one-shot
Linear argument, seamless handoffs, cinematic hero.

Fits: launches, premium products, one strong emotional arc.
Bans: visible chapter index, multiple entry points, tool-like chrome.

## Chaptered editorial
Feels like a designed publication with hard chapter transitions.

Fits: methods, research, founder stories, technical narratives.
Bans: endless full-bleed media under copy, magnetic-CTA finale, generic filmic hero.

## Live surface
The page behaves like the actual product/tool.

Fits: software and interactive systems.
Bans: fake dashboards, decorative marketing chrome, cinematic scrub as the core argument.

## Continuous world
One fixed stage for the entire scroll. The visitor travels through a single place.

Fits: geography, supply chain, construction journey, a physical process.
Bans: visible section seams, independent pinned acts, hard ground changes.

Expensive and fragile. Use only when continuity is the concept.

## Typographic poster
Typography is the main imagery.

Fits: verbal brands, manifestos, campaigns with one sharp claim.
Bans: decorative stock media, card grids, excessive interaction devices.

## Gallery / catalog
Objects are the unit. Labels are factual, not promotional.

Fits: product ranges, portfolios, materials, model families.
Bans: argument-heavy pinned copy, one giant generic hero claim.

## Split stage
Two persistent zones evolve in relation: e.g. technical reality vs finished result, problem vs solution, drawing vs built object.

Fits: architecture, before/after, technical proof, comparisons.
Bans: unrelated full-screen scenes that break the relationship.

## Rhythmic cutlist
Short, sharply different scenes cut with deliberate pacing.

Fits: energetic brands, event/portfolio reels, sequences with many distinct proof points.
Bans: long continuous camera travel and repetitive scroll spans.
